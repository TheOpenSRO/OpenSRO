# Round 736 — crowd scale: spec and measurement

The user wants 1000+ other players on screen and still playable. This round:

- reads what the original does with many characters;
- reads what OpenSRO does for each other player today;
- measures where the frame goes at 20, 200 and 1000 on the box, and what the server spends;
- plans the lanes that will build it.

Nothing changes in play. The only code is QA-only and test-only: a crowd probe and two lineup
switches on the client, and a server benchmark. The spec is `docs/protocol/crowd_scale.md`.

Labels: READ, MEASURED, COMPUTED, INFERRED, OPEN, OURS.

This round is based on round 734's branch (`worktree-agent-a6fa7c97bf5b824d2`, dev plus rounds 734
and 735). Its `EquipFx` and glow are what a 2026 crowd wears, and its round-734 numbers are this
round's starting point.

## Found

1. **Retail never reduces other players** (READ; crowd_scale.md section 1).
   - There is no cap on visible characters and no frame budget.
   - The mesh LOD factor is 0 for an exact CICPlayer.
   - The every-other-frame pose skip applies only to far non-players past 100 characters.
   - Weapon particles draw at every distance (factor 0 against the 0.9 gate).

   What retail saves is two things:
   - **no pose for an object that was not drawn the previous frame** (`0x9c4180` ->
     `CCompChar::FUN_00c33230`, the keyframe sampling behind `vis`);
   - the server's 3 x 3-block interest area with paged group spawns.

   Character Sight Range (case 9) has no reader after six probes. INFERRED dead.

   So **1000 on screen at 60 fps needs OURS techniques**. Retail would pose and fully draw every one.
2. **OpenSRO poses every other player every frame, seen or not** (READ). A player skeleton gets
   `MonsterCrowd.CullCache.NONE`: no frustum test, no range test, no LOD and no hearing cut.
3. **Posing is about 65% of the frame** (MEASURED: pose off, about 196 -> 71 ms at 1000 and 43 ->
   9 at 200). It splits into:
   - the GDScript pose, 63 µs a body;
   - Godot's `Skeleton3D` update, about 20 ms of `TIME_PROCESS` at 1000;
   - the re-skin, about 5 ms of render CPU;
   - the physics-step spiral the slow frame drives: 8 steps a frame against 4.3.

   Each body carries **14 distinct Skins** (`_own_skins`), 6 of them on meshes hidden under armour.
   Godot 4.2.2 writes every bind of every registered Skin on each pose (`skeleton_3d.cpp:245-297`,
   READ). A hidden mesh's skin stays registered (`mesh_instance_3d.cpp:167-180, 330`, READ).
4. **An off-screen crowd costs 86% of an on-screen one.** With the camera turned away (26 of 1000 in
   view) the frame is 168 ms against 196. Retail would pose the 26.
5. **The frame is CPU-bound.** The GPU takes about 5 ms at 1080p with 1000 bodies.
   - The bodies make 7,900 draw calls: about 8 visible meshes a body, one call each.
   - Hiding the meshes takes render CPU 24.7 -> 4.4 ms.
6. **The physics-step spiral is back for players.** At 5 fps a frame runs the capped 8 physics
   steps, and the others' interpolation loop runs in each.
   - A walking crowd adds 40 ms at 1000 (p95 354).
   - One real `OpEntityMoved` a body a second adds 62 ms. The dispatch itself is only 7-10 µs a
     packet (about 1 ms a frame); the rest is the walk the packets start.
7. **Cost per body is linear**, about 0.2 ms. 60 fps with 1000 needs about 10 µs: 20 times less.
8. **The server refuses a player arriving where 167 or more are present** (MEASURED).
   `EnterWorldResult` puts every other player in one frame, which fits 166 full-kit others under
   MaxPayload 32,767, and the session is closed.

   At 1000 in one bucket the server spends about 6 CPU-seconds a second on one move a second each.
   79% of that is one `write` syscall per 26-byte frame. Players have no interest area at all (only
   monsters do).
9. Names, click bodies, circle shadows, the overlay viewport, shadow casting under Circle and the
   run clip are all within the spread.
   - Under Detail the sun's shadow map costs about 10 ms at 1000 and 3.5 at 200.
   - `EquipFx` costs about 9 ms at 200 and 8 at 1000.
   - Clip sounds cost 5 ms of GDScript at 1000.

## Built (QA and test only)

- **`QaCrowdProbe`** (`client/scripts/world/qa_crowd_probe.gd`, new).
  - After `QaLineup` spawns its bodies, it steps through switches, each turning one part of every
    lineup body off.
  - It prints a `QA CROWD step <name> n=.. in_view=.. frames=.. frame_ms=.. p50=.. p95=..
    render_cpu_ms=.. render_gpu_ms=.. process_ms=.. physics_ms=.. idle_ms=.. pose_ms=.. sound_ms=..
    minimap_ms=.. posed_per_frame=.. steps_per_frame=.. draws=.. objects=.. prims=.. nodes=.. fx_*=..`
    line per step.
  - At the start it prints a census line: nodes, meshes, skins, skeletons, bones and distinct
    meshes and materials, for one body and for all of them.
  - The steps are `base`, `pose`, `sound`, `mesh`, `labels`, `collide`, `fx`, `shadow`, `blobs`,
    `overlay`, `run`, `move`, `net`, `netK` (K packets a body a second through
    `NetClient._dispatch`), `away` (camera turned 180°), `nodraw` and `gone`.
  - `gone` must be last: the clip tick drops a skeleton that is out of the tree.
  - `mesh` hides only the meshes that were showing, and shows only those again. Its first version
    showed every mesh, armour-covered body parts included.
  - Environment: `OPENSRO_QA_CROWD_STEPS`, `_SECONDS` (6), `_DELAY` (4), `_QUIT`, `_NET_RATE`.
  - The pose and sound switches reuse round 664's `_qa_split_off`. They also stop the player's and
    the NPCs' poses: a few dozen bodies, the same in every step.
- **`QaLineup`** gains two switches:
  - `OPENSRO_QA_LINEUP_COUNT=<n>` cycles the list to n bodies;
  - `OPENSRO_QA_LINEUP_PLUS=<p>` puts every lineup weapon and shield at +p.

  The `LINEUP:` line now prints the build time.
- **`server/internal/session/crowd736_bench_test.go`** (new, `//go:build linux`):
  - the real `gateway.Server` on loopback;
  - client sockets in a child process that only count;
  - the real `handleMove`, `enterOwned` and leave;
  - `BenchmarkCrowd736HubMove` for the hub lock.

  The smoke test (n = 20) runs in the ordinary suite. It pins 26-byte moves, 203-byte enters and
  14-byte leaves.

## Measured: the server

Run on the workstation (5950X): `cd server && OPENSRO_CROWD736=1 go test -count=1 -p 1 -run
TestCrowd736Measure -v ./internal/session/`. One move a body a second (OURS model).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 12,000 | 2.2 | 19, 0.49 | 0.010 | 2 frames, 4,028 B, 0.14-0.27 ms |

| 1000 | 595,000-614,000 | 5,870-6,580 | 999, 25.97 | 25.97 | refused (170,066 B) |

- The profile at 1000: 79% `syscall.Syscall6` under `gateway.writeFrames`.
- The hub write lock is held 1.3 / 12 / 59 µs per move; 293 KB is allocated per move at 1000.
- Town and field layouts measure the same.

READ, with file and line:

- the move path: `world.go:1698`, `world/world.go:172,403`, re-encoded per recipient at
  `world.go:1774-1775`;
- the arrival: `world.go:1494-1557`, two store reads per present player at `:1509`, one result frame
  at `:1530`;

  encryption; the header is 6 bytes;
- the client's move rate: `MIN_SEND_INTERVAL` 0.05 s (`World3D.gd:842`), 20 a second while walking.

## The plan

Three lanes; crowd_scale.md section 5 has the table with savings.

- **A, client animation and skinning:**
  - A1 pose only what was drawn (READ);
  - A2 one Skin per body;
  - A3 shared poses per (clip, frame);
  - A4 a distance animation LOD for players;
  - A5 a GPU crowd (baked bone textures, MultiMesh) for far bodies, with its own spec round.
- **B, client per-body systems:**
  - B1 the others' interpolation once a rendered frame;
  - B2 Detail shadows for the ten nearest within 300 (READ);
  - B3 the clip sounds' 600 cut for players (READ);
  - B4 an arrival build pump;
  - B5 one mesh per material per body;
  - B6 the `EquipFx` reselect;
  - B7 O(1) uid lookup.
- **C, server:**
  - C1 players in the 3 x 3-block area, with paged 0x3017/0x3019/0x3018 arrivals (READ);
  - C2 encode once and write per drain;
  - C3 destination moves (retail 0xB021);
  - C4 the hub lock and O(1) maps.

The target is proposed and is **the user's to confirm** (OURS):

- 1000 in view at 1080p, Middle, on the box as the proxy: standing p50 at most 16.7 ms, walking p95
  at most 33 ms;
- no arrival frame over 100 ms.

The device's CPU and the 4K case are OPEN. The command to run the probe on the device is in
crowd_scale.md section 4.

## How to rerun

On the box, with your own `RENDER_DIR` and the box lock, from a shipped client
(`RENDER_DIR=... client/tools/remote_godot.sh check`):

```
OPENSRO_MAPM_PATH='97\168.m' OPENSRO_QA_AT=1002,1020 OPENSRO_QA_YAW=0 OPENSRO_QA_LOCAL_NPCS=1 \
OPENSRO_QA_LINEUP=all OPENSRO_QA_LINEUP_COUNT=1000 OPENSRO_QA_LINEUP_PLUS=13 OPENSRO_QA_LINEUP_COLUMNS=40 \
OPENSRO_QA_LINEUP_SPACING=8,10 OPENSRO_QA_LINEUP_PHASE=100000 OPENSRO_QA_DIST=90 OPENSRO_QA_PITCH=-15 \
OPENSRO_QA_CROWD_STEPS=base,pose,sound,labels,collide,fx,shadow,blobs,overlay,run,move,net,away,mesh,nodraw,base,gone \
OPENSRO_QA_CROWD_SECONDS=6 OPENSRO_QA_CROWD_QUIT=1 \
  <client>/tools/xwl_run.sh -s 1920x1080 Godot --rendering-method forward_plus --rendering-driver vulkan \
  --resolution 1920x1080 --path <client> res://scenes/World3D.tscn --quit-after 200000 --print-fps --disable-vsync
```

(plus the usual `OPENSRO_*_PK2_PATH`s). A 1000-body boot takes about 4 minutes and writes a log of
about 700k lines, most of them the per-body build lines. `PHASE=100000` keeps the lineup's own
measurement off: it CPU-skins every body in GDScript.
