# Round 738 — crowd scale, lane A: animation and skinning

The spec is `docs/protocol/crowd_scale.md` (round 736), lane A. The target is 1000 other players in
view at 1080p, Middle: standing p50 at most 16.7 ms and walking p95 at most 33 ms. This round built
A1-A4 and wrote A5's spec (`docs/protocol/crowd_gpu.md`). Lanes B (round 739, per-body systems) and
C (round 737, server) ran beside it.

Labels: READ, MEASURED, COMPUTED, INFERRED, OURS, OPEN.

Base: `3fc71af2` (dev plus round 736).

## Built

**A1, pose another player only while it is in view (READ).**

- The original poses a character only if it was drawn the frame before. The draw sets `+0xbc` on
  passing the frustum test, and `CCompChar::FUN_00c33230` samples keyframes only when it is set,
  while the clip time always runs (READ in round 736: `0x9c4180`, `0x9c4bc4..0x9c4bd3`).
- `MonsterCrowd.CullCache.info` now recognises another player's skeleton by its holder's
  `character_id` (`CrowdAnim.is_other_player`). It gives it a sixth field and a cull radius: its
  meshes' bind-pose reach times its Height, x1.5 (OURS margin).
- `World3D._tick_idle_animations` poses such a body only while that sphere is inside the camera's
  six frustum planes. Its clock and its sounds run on; the sounds are lane B's B3.
- The doll, the screens and the local player are not other players. They are posed as before.

**A2, one skin per body (OURS).**

- `SkinnedBsrAssembler.assemble_from_archive(..., canonical = true)` is used for
  `CharacterAppearance`'s bodies only. NPCs and monsters do not use it.
  - It gives the skeleton its `OBJECT_SPACE_BONE` up front.
  - It makes one Skin naming every bone at the inverse of its global rest
    (`Skeleton3DBuilder.canonical_skin`). That is exactly `build_skin`'s bind for that bone, and
    identity for the object-space bone.
  - It remaps each skinned piece's vertex bone indices from its palette to the skeleton's bones
    (`Skeleton3DBuilder.canonical_mesh`).
- Worn pieces weighted onto the body get the same treatment
  (`attach_item_from_archive(..., canonical)`). Items with their own skeleton keep their skins.
- **One skin per skeleton layout.** `CharacterAppearance.share_template` gives every body template
  of one skeleton file and bone count the same Skin object.
  - Worn items are made once per layout on whichever body wore them first. Before this, a body
    could carry two equal skins, 1.85 a body (MEASURED).
- **Volume** is no longer an in-place edit of per-body skin copies. It is a shared variant per
  scale (`CharacterAppearance.volume_skin`, kept on the base skin).
  - `_own_skins` now copies only per-piece skins skinned to the body skeleton. Item-skeleton skins,
    which Volume never touches, are no longer copied per body.
- `OPENSRO_QA_CROWD_SKINS=own` builds the old way, for the A/B.

**The indexed pose (OURS, round 664's).** `CharacterAppearance.build` puts
`MonsterCrowd.META_LAYOUT` (skeleton file and bone count) on every body skeleton. So the clip tick
finds a clip's bone indices once per layout, and leaves the bone globals to the skeleton's own
end-of-frame update, as it does for a monster. Before, it searched each track by name and forced an
update.

**A3, baked poses (OURS).**

- `CrowdAnim.Frames` bakes a clip's pose, lazily, at multiples of `QUANTUM_MS` = 1000/120 ms. It
  does this once per clip object and skeleton layout. A body takes the nearest frame.
- **Only the moving channels.** A body that stays on its clip is given only the channels the clip
  moves. A body that changes clip is given every channel.
- **Baked poses shared per file, clips not (after the review, F1).** Each body template keeps its
  own clip objects. A clip carries the entry its body found it as (`BsrReader.CLIP_META`), and
  `AnimSounds` looks that body's own sound set up by it. The baked `Frames` are shared by file path
  and layout (`CrowdAnim.frames_for`; `SkinnedBsrAssembler.CLIP_PATH_META`).
  - The round first shared the clip objects themselves: 104 objects became 38. The review measured
    the cost: 169 body pairs whose footsteps came and went with whoever looked the file up last.
- **No reference cycle.** A Frames object keeps copies of the clip's key times, never the clip.
  So a clip parsed for one cast is freed with its frames (tested).
- **Why the quantum is not visible.** The clip clock advances in whole physics steps (1/60 s, two
  quanta; `TickGate` sums them). So within one cycle of a loop a body's offset from its own time, at
  most 4.17 ms, is the same at every pose: a fixed shift of a phase that is itself ours (0.37 s x
  its index).
  - At a loop's wrap the offset is re-rounded unless the duration is a multiple of the quantum.
    That gives at most 0.67 ms of step error once a cycle (MEASURED by the review: 50 of the
    Figures' 75 loops; corrected label, F5).
  - Every pose shown is an exact pose of the clip.
  - MEASURED: the worst bone displacement at the worst offset is 0.44 units (a monk's running
    foot).

**A4, far bodies posed less often (OURS).**

- The rule applies only while the clip tick holds at least 100 characters. That is the original's
  own trigger for its pose skip (`Map::FUN_00bbdb00(count > 99)`, READ), which it applies only to
  far non-players.
- Beyond 900 a body is posed every second frame, beyond 1,800 every fourth, each on its own slot.
- **Not round 664's 300 / 900.** The probe MEASURED the fastest bone at 1.77 units in 1/60 s.
  COMPUTED at 1080 lines and 60 degrees:
  - one skipped pose is 5.5 px at 300 units, a visible change for a mid-range runner;
  - at 900 it is 1.8 px.

**QA.**

- `QaCrowdProbe` has new `anim=<1|i|3|4|->` steps that switch the rules for one step. `anim=-`
  poses another player exactly as before the round (tested).
- It prints the skin census: the skins registered on each body skeleton and the binds one pose
  writes.
- Its step line carries `anim=`, `baked=` and `static_mb=`.

**Probe tool.** `client/tools/crowd_anim_probe.gd` is the real-archive check of A2 and the A3/A4
numbers (`client/tools/README.md`).

**A5: spec only.** `docs/protocol/crowd_gpu.md`.
