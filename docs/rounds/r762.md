# Round 762: a crowd appears together, and leaves without a freeze

The user, in the live swarm crowd on v0.1.514: "players slowly load in. They should all appear on
the screen at once. That might take a lot of data though, see how the client and server do that to
make sure we're compatible and efficient as possible." And, added mid-round: "when I walk away from
the crowd the client locks up really hard and fps dips really low", with the rule for both, verbatim:
**"It should perform that as in the crowd and only get faster, not worse."** -- no frame of a walk
into or out of a crowd slower than the steady in-crowd frame p95 at the same spot.

Labels: **READ** (the retail binaries' decompilation on the box, Godot 4.7.2-stable's source, our
code), **MEASURED** (the render box 192.168.10.78: RX 7600, RADV NAVI33, 28 threads, the Godot 4.7.2
editor build, Forward+ on Vulkan through `xwl_run.sh`, 1920 x 1080, vsync off, every feature at its
default, the separate render thread; no native library on either side), **COMPUTED**, **INFERRED**,
**OURS**, **OPEN**.

Base: dev `886f0473` (round 761). "Before" in every table is that tree with this round's QA only
(the probe's frame log and steps, NetClient's QA counters, the lineup's group bracket and hand-over;
`/opt/opensro-client-r762base`); "after" is `632adf28` (section 5's tables), and `fe3b9778` moves the
two prewarms after the world's entry (section 7's gate; a confirming boot in 5.1). Commits:
`7b1f645e`, `c529eb42` (the first session: the held page, the GPU fade-out, the departure queue, the

## 1. The answer

**Retail builds and sends a whole crowd in one call, and its client creates (or deletes) the whole
group in one handler, every body fading in (or out) together** (section 2, READ). OpenSRO's server
already sent the same bytes the same way; its client did the same work in one frame -- and in the
frame after a group despawn's fade-out, deleted every departed body at once: **the walk-away lock-up
was those frames: 1.7 s live (1,470 leaving), 4.2-7.1 s offline (3,000-4,000)**, with a 0.7-2.0 s
frame for the despawn itself (MEASURED, section 3).

Now a page of arrivals is held at alpha 0, made a frame's share at a time, and **shown in one frame,
every body fading in together**; a leaving crowd is taken apart a frame's share at a time and fades
out on the GPU; and every piece of either draws from **one budget a frame aimed at the scene's own
frame** (section 4).

| (MEASURED; client frames, ms) | before | **after** |
|---|---|---|
| live walk-in, 1,471 arriving: all drawn | 8.3 s, one at a time | **4.9 s, all 1,472 at once** |
| live walk-in: worst frame | 284.6 | **32.0** (an NPC's arrival, 4 frames; section 6) |
| live walk-out, 1,470 leaving: worst frame | **1,675.1** | **15.6** |
| live: steady in-crowd p95 | 39.2 (the dressing never ends) | 10.4 |
| offline 4,000 (cold): all drawn | 60.5 s, one at a time | **14.9 s, all at once** |
| offline 4,000: worst arrival / departure frame | 1,190.5 / **7,129.5** | 39.7 / **21.1** |
| offline 4,000, a warm page: all drawn, worst frame | (no reveal) 246.2 worst | **7.3 s, all at once; 19.7** |

Against the rule (frames over the in-crowd p95 at the spot; the steady frames themselves are over
their own p95 5 % of the time, by definition): **the warm arrivals are at or under that 5 %** (4,000:
0.5 %; 3,000: 1.6 %; 1,000: 3.5 %), **the departures are at 0.5-5.9 %** with their worst frame
1.8-7.5 ms over the p95 (the GPU drawing a whole crowd's 1.5 s fade-out at once), the live walk out
at 8.3 % (+5.2 ms worst), and **the cold arrivals are not there yet**: their first 2-3 s (the
templates being made on the worker) run frames of 30-110 ms, on both trees (section 5, 6).

## 2. What the original does (READ)

### 2.1 The server builds and sends a crowd in one call

`SR_GameServer.exe` (`/var/tmp/decomp_server`, `docs/protocol/pc_spawn.md`, round 737):

- **A full insert** (a world entry, a zone change: CMsgBlock slot 2, `0x534340`) sends the 3 x 3
  window's objects as group spawns, the neighbour blocks first and the object's own block last
  (asm `0x534407`). A walk across a block line sends only the difference blocks: the gained ones as
  group spawns, the lost ones as group despawns.
- **A group** is `0x3017 {u8 kind, u16 count}`, then `0x3019` data frames, then an empty `0x3018`
  (kind 1 spawn: the records; kind 2 despawn: u32 unique ids, `FUN_00533b90`). Each data frame
  carries 4,052 body bytes: the 0x1000 message buffer (`0x471500` / `0x471580`) less the 6-byte
  header, the `0x2209 {u32, u16}` envelope and the 0x20 `FUN_00430a20` keeps back.
- **The order** inside a block: its object maps' order, a `std::map` keyed by the unique id
  (`obj+8`, walked by `FUN_0041fee0`; `FUN_00533910`).
- **No cap and no pacing**: every group of the window is built and handed to the network inside
  the one call. Nothing spreads a crowd over time on the server side.

### 2.2 The client creates (and deletes) a whole group in one handler

`sro_client.exe` (`/var/tmp/decomp_client`; round 756's section 1 has the call chains):

- `0x3017` (`FUN_008ac0e0`) opens a buffer; each `0x3019` (`FUN_008ac170` -> `FUN_00833380`)
  appends to it; neither creates anything. A second `0x3017` before the `0x3018` asserts
  (`0x8abfa0`).
- `0x3018` (`FUN_008ac3d0` -> `FUN_008a6d20`) reads the type byte and the u16 count and loops:
  - **type 1**: `do { FUN_008a6bd0(stream, 0); } while (--n)` -- each record's object created,
    its model and every equipment mesh loaded synchronously (`ResourceMgr`), its 2.0 s appear fade
    started in the parse (vslot 0x6c, round 676). Only textures load on the loader thread.
  - **type 2** (READ this round, `FUN_008a6d20` -> `FUN_008a6cc0` per id): read the u32, clear the
    selection if it is the selected object (`DAT_00eef5ec + 0xf8`), look the object up
    (`IGIDObject::FUN_009c31a0`) and call its vslot 0x4c -- the 1.5 s disappear deco (round 677);
    the object is deleted when that ends.
  - type 0 asserts (`"byListSub"`, `NetProcessInObject.cpp` line 0x45c).
- A single `0x3015` (`FUN_008a6ca0` -> `FUN_008a6bd0(stream, 1)`) and `0x3016` (`FUN_008a6cc0`)
  are the same calls for one object.
- The packet pump drains every message that arrived each tick (`MsgStreamBuffer::FUN_00842ff0`),
  so a whole group is handled in one tick, and a new character is drawn and pickable the frame it
  is made (no "resources ready" test in the draw `FUN_00ba2570` or the pick `FUN_0078ee20`).

So retail shows a crowd **in one frame, every body fading in together over 2.0 s**, after one
long stall for whatever models it has not loaded (INFERRED from the synchronous loads), and takes
a leaving crowd **in one frame**, every body fading out together over 1.5 s.

### 2.3 OpenSRO's bytes and pacing (READ, our code)

- **The record** is retail's, field for field and width for width (`docs/protocol/pc_spawn.md`'s
  table: written by `server/internal/pcspawn`, read by `client/scripts/net/user_spawn.gd`;
  `pcspawn` tests, `user_spawn_test.gd`).
- **The frames**: `npcspawn.GroupFrames(GroupSpawn, n, body, pcGroupChunkRetail)` -- the same
  `0x3017 / 0x3019 x k / 0x3018` with 4,052-byte data frames; `npcspawn.DespawnGroup` for kind 2.
- **The pacing**: `spawnPlayersTo` / `despawnPlayersFrom` (`server/internal/session/playerview.go`)
  build every group of the window and send them in **one `SendFrames` batch** -- as retail, no
  pacing. (The buffs and states follow as their own frames, after the groups: OURS, round 737.)
- **The order** (this round, server): a group's records by unique id (`sortSpawnsByUID`) and a
  despawn group's ids likewise, and the receiver's own block last (`ownCellLast`); the neighbour
  blocks keep OpenSRO's sorted order (retail's is the order its blocks were linked at region load,
  INFERRED). Go: `TestOwnBlockGoesLast`, `TestGroupRecordsByUniqueID`.
- **The volume** (MEASURED live, `QA NET group`): 1,472 players in 199,571 bytes, 1,003 in
  136,015 -- 136 bytes a record. 4,000 players are about 0.54 MB, 134 data frames.

**Where we differ from the original (OURS)**: the server sends as retail does; the *client* reads a
group a frame's share at a time (NetClient `_group_step`, at most the frame's crowd budget), holds
the bodies it makes at alpha 0 and shows them together in one frame (`_hold_step`), and takes a
leaving group apart a frame's share at a time. Retail's single-frame handling is a stall of many
frames' worth: on this client it was a 330-1,370 ms frame for the group spawn alone and a 4.3-7.2 s
frame for a group despawn of 3,000-4,000 (MEASURED, section 5).

## 3. Where the time went (MEASURED, before)

The frame log (`OPENSRO_QA_FRAME_LOG`, a line a frame with FrameSplit's timers) names each long
frame's work. Before (dev `886f0473`):

- **A group spawn in one frame.** The lineup's 4,000 records handed over in one bracket: a
  1,190 ms frame (`p_lineup` 1,788 ms of the physics steps, the ghost pump's first pass inside it).
  Live, a 1,471-player group read in one dispatch: a 246 ms frame.
- **Then every frame a ghost pump over the whole queue.** Nearest first, a key built and sorted for
  every queued body each pass, and the template asks: 300-400 ms frames for about a minute at 4,000
  (`ghosts_ms` 300-350, `g_add` 130-230 ms a frame once kinds were ready); the background dressing

- **A group despawn in one frame** (`left_ms`): 1,971 ms at 4,000, 698 ms live (1,470).
- **The lock-up: every departed body deleted in one frame**, 1.5 s later when their fades ended
  (`post_flush`: the frame-end flush of thousands of `queue_free`s and their render-server frees):

## 4. The design (OURS unless marked)

### 4.1 One budget a frame for a crowd arriving or leaving (`OtherCrowd.FrameBudget`)

Everything a crowd's arrival or departure adds to a frame draws from one budget a rendered frame:
NetClient's reading of a group spawn or despawn (`_group_step`, `_despawn_step`: half of what is
left, 0.3-4 ms), the holders and books of each arrival (inside that reading), the ghosts
(`_other_ghosts.cap_usec`), a held page's activation (`_hold_step`), the departures
(`_on_entity_left` / `_depart_step`), the holders freed after their fade (`_depart_free`), and the
clip baking and rigid pieces a waiting ghost needs (`CrowdGpu.flush`). Each charges what it spent.

- **The aim** is the p90 of the last 120 *steady* frames' own time (the frame less what was charged;
  a frame with a crowd arriving or leaving is not learned from: `World3D._crowd_transition`), and at
  least `TARGET_FLOOR_USEC` (5 ms): a held page draws nothing yet, so the scene it arrives into is
  lighter than the crowd it becomes. The first build learned from every frame and its aim rose to
  30-36 ms in the arrival's first seconds (MEASURED).
- **The budget** is the aim less the last frame's own time, within [1.5, 12] ms: where the scene is
  already at its aim, the work still goes on at 1.5 ms a frame.

### 4.2 A page held and shown whole (round 762's first commit, kept)

A group spawn's bodies are made **ghosts** (round 756: drawn by CrowdGpu from their kind's meshes,
no nodes) at alpha 0 in a hold slot, a frame's share at a time; when every arrived body whose kind
can be drawn is a ghost (or `HOLD_MAX_MSEC` after the page opened: 20 s, for a cold crowd's
templates), the slot is revealed: one shader parameter, every body's 2.0 s fade-in from the same
clock -- retail's group spawn, every body fading in together (READ, section 2.2). Their plates, click
bodies, circles and sounds were made while held (hidden: the plate's anchor, the click body on no
layer) and are switched on nearest first within the budget. Single arrivals (0x3015) are not held:
they fade in as made, as retail's single spawn does.

New this session:

- **The ghosts in arrival order** (`BuildQueue.fifo`): a held page shows whole, so the order it is
  made in is not seen; the nearest-first pass built and sorted a key for every queued id each frame
  (with 4,000 waiting, about half of a 12 ms budget). A pass runs to its end before a reopen starts
  the next from the oldest (a rewind at every template adoption re-asked the oldest waiting ids
  frame after frame).
- **Shown and hidden by layer mask, not visibility** (`CrowdGpu._show_group`): a visibility change
  takes an instance out of the scenario's culling tree and puts it back on the render thread (READ,
  Godot 4.7.2 `RendererSceneCull::instance_set_visible` -> `_unpair_instance` /
  `_instance_queue_update`); the mask is a field the cull tests, and Forward+ pairs geometry with
  VoxelGI only (`geometry_instance_get_pair_mask`), so a mask change unpairs nothing.
- **New bounds 128 groups a flush** (`AABB_PUSH_GROUPS`): every custom-AABB change was a culling
  tree update for every group and fade instance on the render thread -- 50-60 ms of
  `get_frame_setup_time_cpu` (MEASURED, `OPENSRO_QA_FRAME_LOG_RS=1`) -- which the next frame's sync
  waited for (`post_flush` 50-65 ms, the reveal's and the leave's worst frames). Disclosed: a group
  still on the old bounds keeps them for a few frames; a shrink is safe, a growth can leave a part at
  the new edge culled with its group for those frames.
- **The body texture at 4,096 bodies first** (`first_slot_cap`): each growth is a new texture bound
  to every twin (a material update each); a crowd of thousands grew it 256 -> 4,096 in four steps.
- **The twin shaders made ahead** (`CrowdGpu.known_shaders` / `prewarm_one`: the object, glow and
  MTRL mod shaders' twins and fade twins, 20 shaders, about 116 ms in all; one a frame once the
  region grid has loaded, `World3D._prewarm_after_entry`): a page's first ghost made ten
  (`QA TWIN`) and assigning them was 54 ms of a 66-71 ms frame -- `shader_create_from_code`
  compiles on the calling thread under the RenderingDevice (READ, `rendering_server_default.h`
  l. 288-305). The player bodies' template prewarm (the first session's) starts then too: made
  during the load, the two put the perf gate's Jangan entry 1.4 s later (MEASURED, section 7).
- **A rigid piece made a slice at a time** (`rigid_mesh_sliced`, `step_rigid_jobs`): a swarm
  shield's or weapon's vertex loop was a 16.5 ms frame; the ghost waits (as for a clip) instead.
  The same code as `rigid_mesh` in one call (`crowd_gpu_test`: the same vertices, normals, bones).
- **The plates' chunks rebuilt one a frame, once settled** (`NamePlateBatch.flush`): a crowd's plates
  came a few a frame and each rebuilt their 256-plate chunk every frame (w_plates 5-7 ms); a new
  plate is hidden until its chunk is rebuilt (its slot may still hold another plate's glyphs).
- **The dressing only with room** (`_dress_room`): none while a crowd arrives or leaves, and only
  where the crowd's p95 less the last frame leaves room for a dressing as long as the longest seen;
  a trickle tried first (one every 30 frames whatever the room) made 30-34 ms frames in the live
  walk-out (the swarm's kits dress in 3-34 ms, MEASURED). Where the frames leave no room (the box's
  ghost crowds) the ghosts stay undressed -- the same pixels (round 756's parity), less their
  weapons' particles -- unless something needs them dressed (a hit, a cast, an aura, sitting).

### 4.3 A crowd taken apart a frame's share at a time (round 762's first commits, kept)

A departure is taken whole (every book, its plate, circle and click body, its 1.5 s fade-out on the
GPU) while the frame's budget lasts, and queued by id past it; its holder is freed from a queue
when the fade is over (`Node.free` within the budget: a `queue_free` deleted at the frame's end,
outside any budget). A far body fades out where it is drawn (CrowdGpu), never brought back to the
CPU path to fade. The quadratic passes a leaving crowd made (`_recently_left`, EquipFx's nearest
weapons moving onto the next to leave) are gone.

### 5.3 What the rule leaves

- **Warm arrivals** (the crowd's kinds known: the user walking back into a crowd, a crowd walking
  in): at or under the steady 5 % (0.5-3.5 % offline; the worst frame 3-6 ms over).
- **Departures**: 0.5-5.9 % offline, worst 1.8-7.5 ms over; live 8.3 %, worst 5.2 ms over. What is
  left is the render thread drawing a whole crowd's 1.5 s fade-out (`post_flush` 13-15 ms of the
  21 ms frames at 4,000): blended, so no early depth rejection, and every group with a fading body
  drawn twice (its opaque instance skips the fading bodies' vertices, its fade instance the others').
- **Cold arrivals**: the first 2-3 s, while the kinds' templates are made on the worker, run frames
  of 30-110 ms on both trees (section 6.2); after them the hold's frames are at the 5 % line (4,000:
  4.9 % over the whole arrival).
- **Live walk-in**: the NPCs' four frames, and 8 % of frames over (the hold's budget aims at the
  outer spot's frame, 7.5 ms, while the crowd it walks into draws at 10.4).

### 10.1 What changed

- **F1, ghosts carry their weapon particles (OURS, the review's "better" fix).** A recipe now lists
  the particle rows `_apply_glow` would attach (`CharacterAppearance._far_fx` -> `FarRecipe.fx`: the
  effect file and scale, the item's template skeleton and bone, the body bone the item hangs from,
  the item skeleton's transform under its attachment). `World3D._ghost_fx` registers them as EquipFx
  hosts on the ghost's holder (`EquipFx.add_ghost_host`); the driver places each from the GPU clip
  frame (`CrowdGpu.ghost_item_frame`: the body's transform x the baked bone x its rest x the chain,
  `item_frame`'s composition for a far dressed body, round 747) and picks them as any host
  (`ghost_origin`: out of reach while held or fading out). The hosts go when the ghost is dressed
  (the built items' own take over in the same frame), leaves, or is drawn in a new kind.
  `EquipFx.MAX_DISTANCE` is 720 (READ, round 761: LOD 0.9 x 800, `FUN_00c43780`), was 800 (OURS).
  Two costs found on the way: masters made together stepped together (`fx_masters` p95 12.4 ms at
  4,000) -- each master now starts its 50 ms frames at its own phase (OURS); and a page's first ghosts
  made up to eight masters in one ghost (28 ms of one pump) -- a ghost's kinds now get their master a
  frame's share at a time (`_make_masters`, 1 ms, one at least).
- **F1, the background dressing progresses.** Its room is now `max(aim, 16.7 ms)` less the last
  frame's *work* -- wall time less the wait between frames, measured by two marks at the ends of the
  process order (`OtherCrowd.FrameMark`) -- against the whole pass's measured cost, plus a guaranteed
  one body every 600 frames. The review's room was not the only block: a pass that stopped at the
  ghosts for want of room left the queue idle, and it never looked at them again
  (`BuildQueue._room_wait`). The guaranteed rate is one frame in 600 because a background dressing
  frame MEASURED 18-23 ms against a 14-16 ms steady p95 at 4,000; with the particles drawn on ghosts
  the dressing no longer changes what is seen.
- **F2.** `BuildQueue.promote` remembers an id that is not queued (`promote_later` for one held or not
  yet a ghost), and it is urgent when queued after its page; at activation every id with buffs in
  `_target_buff_book` is promoted. `forget` on a departure.
- **F3 / F6, pages.** A page is an `ArrivalPage` with its own hold slot, ghosts and waiting count; a
  group's first share (`NetClient.group_serial`) arriving more than `PAGE_JOIN_MSEC` (3 s, OURS) after
  the current page opened opens a new one; each page is revealed when its own arrivals are ghosts (not
  while its group is still being read) or 20 s after it opened. `_replace_others_for_arrival` resets
  every page (`_reset_pages`: a held slot revealed, nothing left waiting); a deadline reveal drops
  waiting ids whose body is gone and moves the live ones to a page of their own; an arrival that is
  neither a ghost nor queued again (no appearance yet) leaves its page.
- **F4.** `_depart_step` runs before the ghost pump, with a 1 ms floor (`DEPART_FLOOR_USEC`).
- **F5.** While a group is read, NetClient reads on and dispatches what cannot concern a body of the
  group (`_passes_group`: this player's own results and status, chat, the monsters, the ground items,
  a known player's moves none of whose earlier moves wait); the rest waits in order and goes first
  once the group is done; after a held zone change, teleport or revive everything waits.
- **F7.** The free queue is two queues by delay (`_depart_free_now` for an AppearFade model's
