## 1. #3: a part of the crowd missing after a return

### 1.1 What the original does (READ, rounds 664 and 762)

Retail's server keeps players in 320-unit message blocks, each linked to its 8 neighbours; an object
put in a block is sent the 3 x 3 window's objects as one group spawn per block (0x3017 / 0x3019 /
0x3018), the neighbours first and its own block last, all in one call; a walk over a block line
sends the gained blocks' groups and the lost blocks' despawns. The client creates each group whole
in its 0x3018 handler (`FUN_008ac3d0` -> `FUN_008a6d20`), every body fading in over 2.0 s. So a
crowd **beyond** the window is not shown until a crossing brings its block: a square edge, not a
radius. That much is retail's own behaviour, and OpenSRO's server does the same
(`playerview.go`, `TestOwnBlockGoesLast`, `TestSpawnAndDespawnGroupsGoInBlockAndUniqueIDOrder`).
What the user saw went beyond it, in two ways (OURS, both).

(`group spawn of N` is printed when a group is fully read.) Round 762's review (F6) opens a new page
for a group whose **first share is read** more than `PAGE_JOIN_MSEC` (3 s) after the current page
opened; NetClient reads a group a frame's share at a time (`_group_step`, the crowd budget), and a
4,000 window's last groups start being read 3-5 s after its first. So one window, sent by the
server in one batch, became two pages: 3,061 shown, then 939 almost five seconds later; 2,469, then
1,531 more than seven seconds later. In between, one or two blocks of the crowd are missing: the
"chunk that isn't a radius".

**Fix (OURS).** A page is joined by the groups that **arrived** within `PAGE_JOIN_MSEC` of its
first, by their receive time: `PacketReader` stamps each frame with the time of the socket read
that completed it (`Packet.rx_msec`: a frame left in the buffer while NetClient holds its dispatch
back keeps its arrival time), NetClient keeps the 0x3018's as `group_rx_msec`, and World3D's
`_arrival_page` compares those (`ArrivalPage.rx_ms`). A run across block lines (a batch every few
seconds on the wire) still opens a page per batch, as F6 meant. Also: `group_reading()` counts the
group ends held behind the group being read (`_held_groups`), so a page is not revealed in a frame
that falls between two of its groups.

Tests: `packet_reader_test` (`_test_rx_time`: frames drained together keep the drain's time though
cut 60 ms later; a later frame its own), `net_group_hold_test` (`_test_held_groups`), and
`crowd_ghost_test` (`_test_pages`: a group read 4,000 ms after its page opened but received with its
first group joins it; one received 4,000 ms later opens its own).

## 2. #4: seal effects that come and go with the camera

### 2.1 The cause (MEASURED; READ, our code)

EquipFx (rounds 734-763) drew a weapon's effect through a holder: a node per host sharing its master
effect's MultiMesh, placed on the CPU every frame, a draw call per master draw node. Only the
`VISIBLE_BUDGET` = 64 hosts nearest the camera **whose body's origin was inside the frustum** (or
within 30 units) had one, chosen again every cycle -- at 11,600 hosts a cycle is 58 frames
(`SCAN_PER_FRAME_MAX` 200). The 4,000 swarm standing (the step line's new `fx_in_view`, hosts
within 720 units and in view before the cut):

| | hosts | within 720 and in view | drawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| facing the crowd (`base`) | 11,601 | **10,898** | 64 |
| turned away (`away`) | 11,601 | 177 | 63 |

So 0.6 % of what retail draws was drawn, and which 0.6 % depended on the camera: turned, the 64
nearest in the new view take the effects, the ones that left the view lose theirs, and the new ones
appear only when the cycle ends. Retail steps and draws a character's effects while its LOD --
camera distance / 800 -- is under 0.9, i.e. within 720 units, every character (READ, round 761:
`CRTModParticle::FUN_00c779c0`, `FUN_00c43780`). The cut is in EquipFx's choice, before either
simulation, so **the Rust and the GDScript paths both show it** (`OPENSRO_EFP_NATIVE=0`: 64 drawn,
MEASURED).

**Not the culling box, and not round 741's "south east".** The holders' bounds are
`EfpEffect.DRAW_AABB` (400 units in the holder's frame) and never cut a drawn holder; round 741's
report ("lines up if I look directly south east") was the holders placed at the bone's position
only, so an effect's offsets ran along world axes -- fixed there by placing the holder in the
bone's whole frame, a composition the batch below keeps (2.3).

### 2.2 The fix: EquipFxBatch (OURS)

`client/scripts/assets/equip_fx_batch.gd`. A ghost's hosts are drawn by its kind's batch: one
`MultiMeshInstance3D` per (effect file, master draw node) for up to four skeleton layouts, its
MultiMesh with (hosts x the node's stride) instances and no instance data. Its shader is the node's
own (the template, the shared-turn twin, a Link's pipe-turn) with the vertex stage replaced:
instance i is host i / stride and particle i % stride; the host's table (7 texels: slot, bone, row
scale, its layout; the item's rest x chain; that x its bone's pose) finds its ghost in CrowdGpu's
own textures (the body transform, the clip frame's bone, as the ghost's parts are skinned), and
composes `EquipFx.place`'s holder frame (orthonormalised, the row's scale x the body's
cbrt(|det|)); the particle (5 texels, Godot's MultiMesh layout) comes from the master's draw buffer,
a texture per kind (`SrEfpSim.draw_buffer` natively, `EfpEffect.keep_draw_buffers` in GDScript). A
host draws none while its page is held or it fades out, past 720 units or Character Sight Range, or
with its body more than 30 units outside the frustum (OURS, `EquipFx.ALWAYS_NEAR`'s distance). No
host is placed on the CPU; a table changes only when a host comes, goes or is chosen.

**Which hosts (OURS, `EquipFxBatch.HOST_BUDGET` = 500):** the 500 nearest the camera **in any
direction**, the cut found each cycle from the scan's distances -- every host within 720 while there
are fewer, as retail. Turning the camera changes nothing; a crowd's farthest hosts, past the 500th,
show none. Dressed bodies (the player's own, a body dressed near the camera) keep EquipFx's holders.

Costs kept off the frame: the particle texture packed natively in one call a master step
(`SrEfpSim.draw_rows`; built in GDScript it was 0.4 ms of main thread a frame), the host tables
rebuilt one frame in six (`REBUILD_EVERY`), parameters set only when they change (set every frame on 1,094 draws they
cost 12 ms of the render thread's frame setup, MEASURED), a node's stride grows by a quarter and
holds the live count in the texture (a per-step count was a material and MultiMesh update on ~200
draws a frame), a node idle for 10 steps is hidden, and the layouts share a draw (1,492 -> 622
draws).

## 3. #2: the arrival delay

### 3.1 Where the time went (READ, the user's logs; MEASURED, the box)

(v0.1.516); 2,467-4,000 in 3.1-5.7 s (v0.1.515). Every reveal line of a session carries the same

On the box, the warm arrivals' window (`cross`: 1,960 arriving while 1,960 fade out), per body:
about 1.05 ms of main-thread work (L516: 2,040 ms spent for 1,958 bodies; this tree 2,068 for
1,960), of which the name plates 0.26 (`plate_add`, TextServer shaping and the glyph quads), adding
parts to their groups 0.23 (`g_add`), the light under each ghost 0.15 (`g_light`, the navmesh
surface and the shadow byte), geometry and clip checks 0.09, part packing 0.08, the recipe 0.01.

### 3.2 The budget on a GPU-bound frame (READ, our code; COMPUTED)

`OtherCrowd.FrameBudget` (r762): budget = aim (the steady frames' p90) less the last frame's own
time, `own = wall - spent`. When the GPU or the render thread sets the frame, the wall time does
not grow with the main thread's work, so `own = wall - spent` and the next budget is
`spent + (aim - wall)`: it can only repeat what was spent, and when the arrival's frames run at or
over the aim it stays at `MIN_USEC`, 1.5 ms, with the main thread idle for most of the frame. At
the user's ~100 fps, 2,473 bodies in 7.2 s is about 1.5-2 ms of crowd work a frame (COMPUTED, at a
per-body cost of about half the box's: INFERRED from their CPU, not measured); at ~7 ms a frame the
same page would take about 1.5 s (COMPUTED).

**Fix (OURS).** While the frame keeps to its aim (`own <= aim`), the budget is the aim less the
main thread's **work** -- `own` less its wait between frames (`OtherCrowd.FrameMark`: the render
thread's sync, a vsync) -- within [1.5, 12] ms; a frame over its aim falls back to round 762's rule.
`OtherCrowd.FrameBudget.budget_for` (tested: an 11 ms frame with 8 ms of wait leaves 8 ms; no wait,
over the aim, or `OPENSRO_QA_BUDGET_WORK=0` leave the floor; never past 12 ms; a wait longer than
the frame not counted).

**What the box shows.** The box's arrivals are not starved: the offline `back` arrives into an empty
scene (aim at its 5 ms floor), and in `cross` the main thread sets the frame (spent 7.6-8.9 ms a
frame under the old rule, at 1080p and at 3840 x 2160). The rule's own A/B on this round's tree
(`OPENSRO_QA_BUDGET_WORK=0` against the default, one boot each): warm `back` 9.47 -> 9.35 s, `cross`
4.91 -> 4.36 s at 1080p, 3.95 -> 4.01 s at 3840 x 2160 -- no loss, and the gain the user's GPU-bound
frame would show is the mechanism above, not a box measurement. Against v0.1.516:

### 3.3 What retail does, and what is left (OPEN)

Retail makes a whole group in one handler (READ, r756 / r762): one stall, then every body fading in
together. "Within a frame or two of its group's packets" would need the per-body work above about
two orders of magnitude cheaper. **OPEN, for the next round:** the ghost's making in Rust -- the
recipe's geometry and clip checks, the part packing into the groups' buffers, the light lookup (the
navmesh surface and the shadow byte, by position), the plate's glyph quads (its shaping stays
TextServer's) -- one pass over a page's bodies on the pool, with the GDScript kept as the fallback
and held to it. Not this round: the cause on the user's machine is the starved budget (3.2); the
per-body cost is what remains after it.

frame -- a plausible share of 106 -> 90 fps that this box cannot show (here the steady crowd never
leaves room).
