# Round 757: the client is main-thread bound in big crowds

The user, live, in a crowd of 4,000 to 7,000 players on a Windows desktop (32 threads, RTX 5080):
"sub-30 fps ... but it's only using 3% cpu, 5.1GB of RAM, and 10-13% of the GPU". 3 % of 32
threads is one core: the main thread. Their own log showed World3D's script work at 16-18 ms a
frame and a wall gap of 100-200 ms, on 4.2.2 and on the 4.7.2 spike alike.

Based on `155822c1` (dev). Labels: **READ** (Godot 4.2.2-stable source, our code), **MEASURED**
(the render box), **COMPUTED**, **INFERRED**, **OURS**, **OPEN**.

## 1. What was found

The frame was not slow in our scripts. It was slow in the engine, called on our behalf, on the
main thread, and multiplied by Godot's step spiral (a 130 ms frame runs 8 physics steps).

| # | The cost | Where (READ) | What it cost at 7,000 (MEASURED) | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **18,035 `Skeleton3D`s each got a physics-step callback.** `Node.duplicate()` copies `animate_physical_bones` (default true) through its setter, which turns the skeleton's internal physics process on outside the editor. The callback walks the bones looking for `PhysicalBone3D` children; we have none. Every body and item skeleton is a template's duplicate. | `skeleton_3d.cpp` l. 303-315, 660-671 | About 5 ms a physics step outside every script (`phys_cb` less World3D's own); 40 ms a frame at 8 steps | `Skeleton3DBuilder.no_physics_step` on every built skeleton: the census then shows none |

**How the costs were named.**

- `FrameSplit` (round 748) splits the frame into its phases and our own timers. Round 757 adds

  `p_minimap`, `w_plates`, and the engine's monitors on the step line (`phys_active`,
  `phys_pairs`, `phys_islands`, `nav_ms`, `objects_total`).
- The probe's census now counts every node that physics-processes or processes, by class. Before
  the fix it printed `{ "Node": 2, "Node3D": 1, "Skeleton3D": 18035, "SpringArm3D": 1 }`; after,
  `{ "Node": 2, "Node3D": 1, "SpringArm3D": 1 }` (MEASURED).
- **Native stacks.** The box's container cannot run `perf` (the host's `perf_event_paranoid` is
  4, MEASURED). Round 757 built Godot 4.2.2-stable from source with symbols (`template_release
  production=yes lto=none debug_symbols=yes`, 6 minutes at -j10) and sampled the main thread's
  stack with `eu-stack` four times a second (`client/tools/main_thread_stacks.py`). After the
  fixes, at 7,000 standing: about 50 % of samples in script callbacks
  (`SceneTree::_process_group` -> `GDScriptFunction::call`), 35-40 % in
  `RenderingServerDefault::_draw` (`RenderForwardClustered::_render_list` the largest leaf), 13-15
  % in `SceneTree::physics_process`. 7.5 % went to `RenderingDeviceVulkan::get_memory_usage`, which
  the probe's own per-frame render monitors call: a QA cost, not the game's.
- **The QA binary is the editor build.** The box's `Godot_v4.2.2-stable_linux.x86_64` (every
  table here, as in every earlier round) is the editor; the game ships as `template_release`. One
  7,000 boot on the symbolized `template_release` build ran 17.1 ms a frame standing, but on a
  code state between this round's measured ones, so the difference between the two binaries is
  not measured here (OPEN).

**The `bone_pose_changed` errors** (the user's 30,382 on 4.2.2, 0 on 4.7.2): not reproduced on the
box. The probe gained `churn` (a body leaves and comes straight back), `redress`, `reglow`,
`leaveall` and `zone` steps, and an audit (`OPENSRO_QA_CROWD_ATTACH_AUDIT=1`) that counts every
in-tree `BoneAttachment3D` against its skeleton's `bone_pose_changed` connections (the state that
error needs, READ `bone_attachment_3d.cpp` l. 143-183). At 300 bodies through every step, 450
attachments against 450 connections each time, 0 errors (MEASURED). The swarm's kits (seal items,
avatars, shoulder pieces) were not reproduced: the box has no Postgres for `cmd/swarm`. OPEN, with
the audit as the tool. The coordinator's 4.7.2 numbers say the errors were not the frame's cost.

**The `!is_geometry_instance` errors** (40 a boot on the box, every boot, base and head; the
user's 274): traced with unbuffered QA prints (`OPENSRO_QA_CROWDGPU_LOG=1`) to before CrowdGpu's
first AABB write, so not CrowdGpu's groups as round 748 inferred. The cause (READ, MEASURED):
`Node.duplicate()` sets a node's properties base class first, so a `MultiMeshInstance3D`'s
`custom_aabb` (GeometryInstance3D) is set before its `multimesh`, on an instance with no base yet,
and the RenderingServer refuses it (`renderer_scene_cull.cpp` l. 1016-1019). CrowdGpu's layout
scratch was a `duplicate(0)` of a live body, weapons' particle nodes included: 20 errors a layout,
two layouts. Fixed twice over: the scratch is the bones alone (`CharacterAppearance.
_bare_skeleton`), and EfpEffect's nodes and EquipFx's holders give their bounds to the
RenderingServer rather than as a stored property, so no duplicate (the portrait's, a redress's)
copies one. MEASURED after: 0 in a 1,000 boot and in each 300 s stress boot.

## 2. Threads

### Rendering on its own thread: crashes on 4.2.2 (left off)

`--render-thread separate` (the project's `rendering/driver/threads/thread_model` 2) crashed every
boot at the world's load (MEASURED, 1,000 and 20 bodies; the 4.7.2 spike lane saw the same on
4.2.2). Under gdb on the symbolized build the crash is an unbounded recursion on the render thread
(MEASURED backtrace, READ source):

```
RenderingServerDefault::_draw            rendering_server_default.cpp:73  emit frame_pre_draw
 -> AnimatedTexture::_update_proxy       animated_texture.cpp:82  (connected to frame_pre_draw)
 -> RenderingServerDefault::texture_proxy_update   rendering_server_default.h:190
 -> CommandQueueMT::flush_if_pending -> _flush      command_queue_mt.h:404, 373
 -> RenderingServerDefault::_draw (the next queued draw) ... again, until the stack is gone
```

Our water is an `AnimatedTexture` (`World3D._load_real_water_animation`). On the main thread the
proxy update is a plain call; on the render thread it flushes the command queue, which holds the
next frame's draw. So threaded rendering stays off, as the coordinator asked; the evidence is for
the 4.7.2 spike, where the command queue was rewritten (OPEN there). What the client would need
beyond that, found while preparing it:

- `frame_pre_draw` / `frame_post_draw` are emitted on the render thread (READ, l. 71-120): a
  script connected to them, or awaiting them, runs there. `FrameSplit` no longer listens to them
  when the render is threaded (`FrameSplit.render_threaded()`), and the probe reads the
  RenderingServer's getters on one frame in 30 (each would wait for the render thread). The QA
  screenshot paths that `await RenderingServer.frame_post_draw` (CharacterSelect's,
  `QaCrowdProbe._ab`) are QA only and are not changed.

### Physics on its own thread: not tried

`physics/3d/run_on_separate_thread` makes direct space queries outside the physics frame an error,
and the client makes them from `_process` (the walkers' and the circles' ground rays). After the
fixes `phys_engine` is 0.6 ms at 7,000 (MEASURED): nothing to move.

### WorkerThreadPool

- **Built: the walkers' landing heights** (`HeightBatch`, 3.4), a group task over the frame's
  points. Engine-independent: plain data in, one index of `out` per task, no Node off the main
  thread, no `const` Array (`nav_graph.gd` and `nvm_collision.gd` are in
  `const_array_lint_test`'s list). Nothing in it dodges a 4.2.2 bug.
- **Not built, measured too small after section 3:** the leg stepping (`o_legs` less heights:
  0.4 ms at 7,000 with 2 % walking), CrowdGpu's texel packing (only moved bodies are packed),
  plate placement (a texel per moved body), packet decode (`NetClient._dispatch` with the walk
  start: 0.32 ms a frame at 7,000 x 2 %, MEASURED). Each is a few hundred microseconds of
  per-element GDScript whose gather and apply would cost as much as the work.

## 3. Built (all OURS unless marked)

### 3.1 Name plates batched (`world/name_plate_batch.gd`)

Other players' plates are drawn as chunks of 256 plates, one mesh each, one surface per
(glyph texture, pass): at 7,000 about 28 chunks instead of 14,000 labels.

- **The same glyphs.** Each plate is shaped with Label3D::_shape's TextServer calls and the
  Label3D's own settings, and each glyph is the same quad with the same UVs into the same font
  cache texture (`_generate_glyph_surfaces`, READ).
- **The same material.** BaseMaterial3D's generated code for get_material_for_2d's flags
  (unshaded, alpha, billboard keeping scale, fixed size, no depth test), written out
  (`NamePlateBatch.shader_code`: 4.2 does not expose a material's generated code to scripts), with
  one change: the billboard turns about the plate's point, read from an anchor texture by the
  vertex's slot (UV2.x). Outline before text by render priority, as Label3D's.
- **The 300 rule (READ, round 673) is the shader's**, exact every frame (the Sweep re-tested a
  sixth of the plates a frame).
- **A plate moves by a texel write** (`World3D._other_moved`), and a spread re-read catches what
  is not reported; an unchanged anchor is not uploaded.
- The Label3D stays as the plate's record (text, look), hidden: the code that reads it, and the
  tests, are unchanged. `OPENSRO_QA_PLATE_BATCH=0` draws Label3Ds as before.
- OURS, disclosed: overlapping plates of one pass draw in slot order, not back to front.
- The look: `QaCrowdProbe`'s `plateab` step saves the same held frame batched, batched again
  (noise floor) and as Label3Ds for `frame_ab.py`. Section 4 has the numbers.

### 3.2 Minimap signs batched (`ui/hud/minimap_signs.gd`)

One MultiMesh a kind (npc, monster, other), drawn by one `draw_multimesh` each. Each instance
carries its entity's world (x, z); the canvas shader applies HudMinimap's rule (READ, vslot_013:
an 8 x 8 quad at `sign_offset`, dropped at SIGN_RADIUS) from the player's place and the zoom
(uniforms). Other players' signs are keyed and written when the body moves; NPCs and monsters
are refilled whole each frame as before. OURS: the kinds stack in a fixed order.

### 3.3 Event-driven per-frame work

- **`_other_moved(body)`**: every path that moves or turns another player's holder reports it
  once; CrowdGpu, the minimap and the plates consume the set. Each keeps a spread re-read for a
  path that does not report (MINIMAP_RESCAN_FRAMES 60; CrowdGpu's REFRESH_FRAMES 15 -> 60).
- **The eased walk looks only at awake bodies** (`_other_awake`): a target write or a cleared
  settled mark wakes a body, a settled body sleeps; a spread re-check (OTHER_WAKE_SCAN_FRAMES 30)
  wakes one moved behind the set's back.
- **Far bodies' sounds from a timing wheel** (0.1 s buckets of the clip clock) instead of a pass
  over every heard body a frame; a spread re-check behind it.
- **CrowdGpu's cycle**: the scan stores packed arrays (id, distance, flags, skeleton) and the
  far bodies' bounds, so the cycle's end is one pass over packed data, not a Dictionary of
  Arrays and a pass over every far body. The in-view count (QA only) is taken only while the
  probe's split runs. `_crowd_eligible` looks through a body's children only when it has more
  than its three (skeleton, plate, click body).
- **Scans capped a frame** (OURS): CrowdGpu's near/far scan and EquipFx's host scan read at most
  200 a frame, the circles' nearest-ten pass 300, so a very large crowd's cycle is longer instead
  of each frame's scan bigger (7,000: CrowdGpu about 0.6 s, EquipFx about 0.8 s, circles about
  0.4 s at 60 fps, against 10, 10 and 6 frames).
- **EquipFx**: the frustum's planes once a slice (Camera3D.is_position_in_frustum builds them on
  every call, READ `camera_3d.cpp` l. 669-677), the distance test before the walk up the tree,
  and the candidates pruned to the 64 nearest hosts (ties kept: every host the cycle can show is
  among them).

### 3.4 Heights on the pool (`world/height_batch.gd`)

`_step_other_legs` collects the frame's points (budget OTHER_HEIGHT_BUDGET 128, OURS, was the
monsters' 32), reads the loaded regions' transforms once, hands each point's graph, local point
and region transform to `HeightBatch`, which runs `NavGraph.landing_height_at` as a group task
(inline under 8 points), and applies the heights after. A point with no loaded graph takes the
terrain ray on the main thread as before. `landing_height_at` reads an object's arrays once, not
three times a triangle. `QaCrowdProbe`'s `inlineh` step runs the same inline for the A/B.

MEASURED: with the heights on the pool, `o_leg_h` (the whole height stage on the main thread:
finding each point's region, the hand-over, the wait, the apply) is 0.59 ms a frame at 7,000
with 2 % walking, and the pool's threads 0.13-0.31 cores (`others`). Before the regions'
transforms were read once a frame, the stage was 2.81 ms on the pool against 2.85 inline (one
diagnostic boot, the `inlineh` step): the main thread's search for each point's region, not the
height, was the cost. The A/B was not repeated after that change (OPEN): how much of the 0.59 ms
the pool saves against inline is not measured.

### 3.5 Skeletons without the physics step (`Skeleton3DBuilder.no_physics_step`)

Called on every skeleton `build_skeleton3d` makes (bodies, items, monsters, NPCs, map objects);
the duplicates inherit it. A 4.2.2 engine behaviour, not a project workaround.

**Both engines, MEASURED** (`client/tools/skeleton_process_bench.gd`, headless: 18,035
skeletons of 49 bones duplicated from one template and added to the tree, as the 7,000 lineup has
them; two marker nodes at the ends of the physics and process orders time every callback
between them):

| | physics callbacks a step | process callbacks a frame | wall a frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.2.2, as built | **5.15 ms** | 0.00 | 6.9 (the headless pace) |
| 4.2.2, `no_physics_step` | 0.002 ms | 0.00 | 6.9 |
| 4.7.2, as built | 0.001 ms | **2.52 ms** | **38.5 ms** |
| 4.7.2, `no_physics_step` | 0.001 ms | 2.31 ms | 33.9 ms |
| 4.7.2, internal process off too | 0.003 ms | 0.001 ms | 6.9 ms |

A fresh `Skeleton3D` does not physics-process on 4.2.2; a `duplicate()` does
(`physics_internal=true`). On 4.7.2 every `Skeleton3D` in the tree processes every frame
(`process_internal=true`, fresh or duplicated), and with 18,035 of them the frame is 38.5 ms of
wall time against 6.9: the callbacks (2.5 ms) and what they set off after the process
callbacks (the rest, INFERRED: the skeletons' deferred updates). **That is the same class of
cost on the spike, per frame instead of per step, and very likely why the user saw the same frame
rate there.** OPEN for the spike lane: turning a skeleton's internal process off on 4.7.2 for the
skeletons nothing poses there (CrowdGpu's far bodies, every item skeleton) -- not tried here,
since on 4.7.2 that process also runs a posed skeleton's modifiers.
