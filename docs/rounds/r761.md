# Round 761: weapon and seal effects in big crowds -- the pool decides the cap as the main thread did

Round 759 moved EquipFx's effect masters onto WorkerThreadPool (`EfpSimBatch`). Its review's F1
found that under the global particle cap the pool gave each effect an even share of the room
left at the batch's start, so in the swarm the particles went to different effects than on the
main thread (600 of 600 frames differed at 33 sims, 15 fps; `system_raretype_a_step3` 87 -> 32,
`eternity_11_nova_bow_03` 3 -> 16), and the share's remainder was never handed out (the pool sat
2-3.5 % under the cap). This round makes the pool's result the main thread's, frame for frame,
reads what the original client limits, and measures what the cap is worth.

Based on dev `21fbab4d` (v0.1.514, Godot 4.7.2). Labels: **READ** (`sro_client.exe` through the
box's decompilation dump `/var/tmp/decomp_client` and its objdump; our code), **MEASURED** (the
render box 192.168.10.78: RX 7600 RADV, 28 threads, Godot 4.7.2 editor build; crowd boots at
1920 x 1080 Forward+ through `xwl_run.sh`), **OURS**, **OPEN**.

## 0. Summary

- **The fix.** A threaded batch now decides every spawn with the main thread's own test: effect
  i's spawn at its own change d is taken iff `base_i + d < limit_i`, base_i being live_total
  after every effect before it in the list has advanced. The batch knows base_i exactly once
  those effects have finished, and before that from bounds each publishes when it starts
  (`EfpSim.growth_bounds`); a spawn the bounds cannot decide waits for the effects before it.
  No share, so no remainder: the room goes in node order, to the last particle.
- **Equality** (MEASURED): the review's probe over the real EquipFx files, 600 frames, per sim
  count / spawned / dropped -- **0 frames differ** at 8 sims 37 fps, 33 at 15 and 60 at 30
  (was 0 / 600 / 599); the new suite's synthetic effects (count, spawned, dropped, positions)
  0 frames differ at 8 / 33 / 60 with the cap binding, a reserved effect, effects held outside
  the batch and a 0.3 s hitch; a second pool run is the first.
- **The cost:** the probe's pool is 1.6-1.9x faster than the main thread where the cap binds
  (round 759's share: 2.0x). In the swarm at 3,000 the frame is the same (median of boots
  13.6 against 13.5 ms) and EquipFx's masters 0.6 ms a frame slower than round 759's (3.5
  against 2.85; section 4). The perf gate: SAME on all four numbers. Speculating past an undecided spawn (with a cheap take-back of the rest of
  the step) was built and measured slower than waiting, and dropped (section 2.3).
- **The original has no particle cap** (READ, section 3): particles come from a free list that
  grows by 8,192 and never refuses; nothing counts particles or effects. Its only crowd thinning
  is per character: a character's effects are neither stepped nor drawn past LOD 0.9 (camera
  distance / 800, so 720 units). The emission-quality globals are set only by a `PLOD` console
  command.
- **The cap stays** (MEASURED, section 4): without it the swarm's effects hold 2,400-2,600
  particles instead of 1,400, and the frame grows by about 6.5 ms at 3,000 (13 -> 20 ms, p95
  18 -> 29) and 6.3 ms at 7,000 (17.5 -> 23.6, p95 24 -> 34); the masters 3.3 -> 8.8 ms.

## 1. What F1 was

`EfpSimBatch.run` (round 759) gave each sim `max(limit - live_total, 0) / n` particles of net
growth, and `EfpSim.can_spawn` refused on that share alone, on every batch. The main thread had
advanced the effects one after another, each spawn taken while the shared live_total was under
the cap: first come first served in node order. The share is not that order, it is not even
(an effect's own deaths free room only for itself, so bursty effects lose to steady ones), and
`room % n` was never given out.

## 2. The fix (OURS)

### 2.1 The rule

On the main thread effect i's spawn sees live_total = L0 + (the whole advance's change of every
effect before it) + (its own change so far): L0 is live_total when the batch starts, and nothing
else touches live_total during it. So effect i's decisions depend only on base_i = L0 +
sum(change_j, j < i), and with base_i known the threaded advance of effect i is exactly its
main-thread advance. `EfpSimBatch.Job` keeps, under its mutex:
- the finished prefix (effects 0..k-1 all done) and live_total after it;
- each effect's change once it finishes;
- each running effect's bounds (least, most), published by the effect's thread when it starts.

`EfpSim.can_spawn` in a deferred advance takes a spawn at once while its change is below
`_sure_below`, refuses it at `_sure_from` or above, and otherwise asks `Job.admit`, which turns
what is known of base_i into new sure limits:
- lower: prefix + finished changes + each running effect's `least`;
- upper: the same with `most`, and base_(j+1) <= max(base_j, limit_j) -- a spawn is only ever
  taken below the limit, so an effect can raise live_total to its limit but not past it.
If the spawn is still undecided, admit waits (20 us steps, looking again when another effect
has finished). The effects are claimed in list order, so every effect before i is already
being stepped, and the first unfinished one never waits: no deadlock.

### 2.2 The bounds (`EfpSim.growth_bounds`)

Read off the sim's state before its advance, without changing it:
- **steps**: advance()'s own count (the same float arithmetic);
- **least**: one step: minus the particles queued to die (only the flush at the step's start
  kills; deaths queued in the step go at the next); more steps: minus every particle;
- **most**: at most MAX_PARTICLES minus the count, and at most the spawns minus the queued
  deaths. Spawns in the first step: per emitter whose owner survives the flush and whose timing
  fires (emit_spawns' tests, the owner's age unchanged until after the emission), at most
  `max_live - ftol(max(acc - its dying, 0))` (acc after the flush's give-backs, each
  `max(acc - 1, 0)`) and at most `ftol(per_trigger) + 2`; times 1 + what its newborns' emitters
  can spawn in the same frame (Template.kids, compiled once). Later steps: every surviving
  emitter again, plus `fan_child` per particle spawned so far.

The particles queued to die are counted as they are queued (`_dying_n`, `Emitter.dying`). The
tests hold the bounds against every real change (`efp_sim_batch_test._bounds`, and the batch
counts `misses`: 0 in every suite and probe run).

### 2.3 Tried and dropped: guessing

The waits are real dependencies while the cap binds: whether effect i may grow depends on
whether the effects before it refilled. I built speculation for the advance's last step: an
undecided spawn guessed from an estimate, the rest of the step kept for taking back (the
emission's place, each later emitter's acc / done / tail, the RNG, the counters, the older
particles' ages: all cheap, since a newborn's frame-0 commands write only the newborn), and an
ordered settle that re-ran a wrong guess exactly. It held equality in every test, guessed wrong
on 1-17 % of effects, and was slower than waiting in the probe (MEASURED, 60 sims 30 fps:
5.45 against 5.18 ms a frame; 33 at 15: 8.78 against 8.52). Dropped.

### 2.4 Why the pool does not scale better (MEASURED, `tools/pool_scale_bench.gd`)

Each mode's loop 5 times on the main thread, then once on each of 5 pool threads (speed-up, 5
is perfect), Godot 4.7.2 editor build on the box:

| construct | speed-up |
|---|---|
| integer arithmetic, Vector3 / Basis, PackedFloat32Array writes, the script's own static var | 3.8-4.6 |
| any GDScript function call: own static, own method, another script's static (`EfpReader.i32`) | 0.6-1.4 |
| `new()` of an inner class | 0.4-1.2 |

So the masters' simulation, a GDScript call per particle and command, takes about 3x its
single-thread time in thread time on 5 threads (the probe: 13.5 s of thread time against 4.6 s
on one thread, 60 sims). This is round 759's pool too. OPEN: whether an exported release
template (no debug call accounting) scales better. The new code keeps GDScript calls out of
its hot loops (EfpReader.i32 and the timing test inline in growth_bounds; the waiting loop
inline).

### 2.5 Smaller

- `EfpSim.limit_for(reserved)` and `cap_total`: the cap in force; `OPENSRO_QA_EFP_CAP=<n>` (QA;
  0: none) for the measurements below.
- EquipFx's `QA CROWD split` gains `fx_cap_waits_calls`, `fx_cap_wait`, `fx_cap_bounds` and
  `fx_cap_live_calls` (live_total after the batch).
- `client/tools/efp_cap_probe.gd`: the review's probe, kept, with the waits, the bounds' and
  the simulations' thread time.
- No new worker file: everything a pool thread runs is in `efp_sim.gd` / `efp_sim_batch.gd`,
  both already in `const_array_lint_test`'s WORKER_FILES; `efp_sim_batch_test` fails if either
  (or efp_reader / efp_effect) leaves the list.

## 3. The original's limits (READ)

Grepped from the box's decompilation dump (43,562 functions, failed=0) and objdump of
`sro_client.exe`; no Ghidra run, no game data copied.

- **No global particle or effect limit.** The emission (`0x00c84e70`) spawns through
  `FUN_00c86cd0(emitter, n)`: `do { p = FUN_00c86c30(); ... } while (--n)`, no count test.
- **The allocator never refuses.** `FUN_00c86c30` (pool `0x1114e80`) pops a free list; when it
  is empty `FUN_00425400` -> `FUN_00425470` mallocs another chunk -- 0x2000 = 8,192 elements of
  0x248 bytes (`IMemory::FUN_0043be40(..., 0x248, 0x2000, 1, 0)` from the pool's constructor at
  `0xd7ae80`). Freeing (`FUN_00c866a0`) appends to the list. The alloc / free counter hooks
  (`FUN_00c86eb0`, `FUN_00c86da0`) are empty. Nothing reads the pool's counters.
- **No per-effect limit** beyond the emitter's max_live: `CRTModParticle::FUN_00c77950` keeps a
  character's effects in a std::vector grown without a cap; `CCompChar::FUN_00c30c20` adds
  without a count check.
- **The emission-quality globals** `DAT_01114d54/58` (EFP_FORMAT.md) have 7 references: 5 writes
  in the setter at `0x547450`, 2 reads in the emission. The setter's only reference
  (`0x558b23`) registers it as the console command `L"PLOD"` (`0xd88e4c`) in `FUN_00558910`,
  beside GM / debug commands (Invincible, MakeItem, ToTown, ShowCLOD). The globals are in .bss,
  so a normal session runs at full quality. The Options window's "Effect quality" is another
  setting (option 0xd: ALL_OFF / DAMEGE_ON / ALL_ON, `FUN_005fef70`, `CIFOption_Video::Create`)
  that does not touch them; what it drops is OPEN.
- **The one crowd thinning: per character, by distance.** `FUN_00c43780` returns 0 when the
  mod's threshold (+0x3c, 0.9 for particles: `CRTModParticle::FUN_00c779c0`, `_DAT_00de3148`)
  is below the character's LOD (`CCompChar` +0x7c); `CRTModParticle::vslot_002 / 003 / 010`
  test it first, and vslot_003 is where the effect is stepped (`FUN_00c81450` ->
  `FUN_00c80ff0`) and drawn. So past it a character's effects freeze (kept, not freed). The LOD
  is the camera distance / 800 (`GlobalDataManager::FUN_009c4aa0`, the double at `0xd83080`),
  so 720 units, unless a per-object rescale applies (`cfg+0x134 x scale + cfg+0x138`, defaults
  OPEN).

EquipFx's own view limit (nearest 64 shown hosts within 800 units, round 734) is OURS and is
not this rule; matching retail's 720-unit freeze is a separate question (not this round).

**So the global cap is ours** (round 620: "~7 us a particle-frame in GDScript ... <= ~14 ms a
50 ms frame"). The reason stands: the simulation is GDScript, the crowd's effect files want far
more particles than one frame can afford, and retail never had to pay for them in a script.
